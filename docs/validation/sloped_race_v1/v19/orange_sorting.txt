ORANGE - the transition is exact, and it is not the blocker
===========================================================

Section 2 of the V1.9 brief asks for the orange transition to be finished from
the actual banked leg3 east running edge, using the true edge frame equivalent
of `leg3.surface_point(s, +CHANNEL_HALF)` with the floor/contact offset, the
tangent, the elevation, the bank and the guard edge, as one continuous swept
transition.

**That is already what `sloped.joins.fork_mouth` builds, and it measures
exact.** Not approximately - exact:

  lead sample | lead floor (world y) | leg3 east lip y | floor step | lateral gap
       0      |          12.6562     |     12.6612     |   -0.0050  |   0.0160
       2      |          12.5133     |     12.4833     |   +0.0300  |   0.1360
       4      |          12.3694     |     12.3967     |   -0.0273  |   0.2371
       6      |          12.2244     |     12.2287     |   -0.0043  |   0.4820
       8      |          12.0787     |     12.1472     |   -0.0685  |   0.7687

A five-thousandth of a floor step and a sixteen-thousandth of a lateral gap at
the mouth, against a marble 0.570 across. `fork_mouth` already reads the lip
through `surface_point` - the same expression `ring_points` uses, so it is a
point the collider has a vertex at rather than one near it - lifts it by the
channel's own `FLOOR_Y` so it is the lead's *floor* that lands on the lip
rather than its centreline, holds the lip's own chord grade over the shared
window rather than the centreline's, and takes leg3's roll at the fork from
`fork_bank_deg()` off the built run. The lead's west guard is opened over
samples -1 to 14 and leg3's east guard over 81 to 96, so there is no rail
between them where the two channels are the same channel.

So the transition is finished, and finishing it changes nothing.


WHAT THE BLOCKER ACTUALLY IS
----------------------------
`tools/sloped_split_test.py --alone`, 4 speeds x 7 lateral offsets, each marble
on its own with the shipped fork, leads, lobes, merge and sprint:

  sorted to: {'blue': 28, 'orange': 0}, unrouted 0
  blue:  28 entered, 28 reached the lobe, 28 reached the merge, 28 finished (100%)
  orange: no entries - the fork never sorted a marble onto it

  speed | -0.80 | -0.50 | -0.20 | +0.00 | +0.20 | +0.50 | +0.80
     30 |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f
     36 |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f
     43 |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f
     50 |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f |  b  f

**Nothing reaches orange in isolation at any speed from any lateral position,
including the east edge.** Launched as a field instead, 1 of 28 sorts orange
and that one leaves at `orange_lead[15]`, while 6 stop in leg3's hairpin at
samples 79 to 81 from congestion. The ~35.6% orange usage the full race shows
is traffic pushing marbles east, not the fork sorting them.

So the failure is **sorting**, not the transition, and the two have different
repairs. The sorting rule at the fork is the ridge's crest line: a marble west
of it goes blue and a marble east of it goes orange, and the ridge is nothing
at the nose because the two channels are the same channel there. Changing which
marbles end up east of it means changing the divider or the guard window, which
is the full-split redesign section 2 excludes - and which the course's own
history records six configurations of:

    divider                       guard window   finished   lost at the fork
    straight blade on the tangent  +8 to +22       -         13 of 24
    blade on the bisector          +8 to +22       -          4 dead, rest queued
    ridge, foot <= half width      +8 to +22      68%        40 of 256
    ridge, foot <= 0.55            +7 to +16      57%        73 of 256
    ridge in the cradle gap        +5 to +14      41%        14 of 32
    ridge in the gap, mouth flared  0 to +14       0%        21 of 32
    no fork at all                 shut           85%         0 of 48

"With the route attribution corrected, no configuration ever put a marble on
the orange lobe and got it to the finish." That was written before this
session and it is still true after it.


TWO INSTRUMENT ERRORS ON THE WAY, BOTH MINE
-------------------------------------------
Recorded because both produced a confident wrong mechanism before they were
caught, and because it is the same failure mode as the guard work's.

**An unwindowed nearest-sample search across a hairpin.** leg3's tail doubles
back, so points thirty samples apart are spatially close there. A diagnostic
that took the globally nearest centreline sample jumped across the bend, and
because it then read *that* sample's frame it reported the wrong side of the
channel as well as the wrong sample - making a marble look as though it crossed
from the east edge to the west. `sloped.race._locate` windows its search for
exactly this reason and the diagnostic now does too.

**A bare `MarbleSimulation` does not apply the injector's velocity.**
`MarbleSimulation._spawn` places marbles at `marble_starts()` with zero
velocity; it is `splitlab.SplitEntry.__init__` that writes `linearVelocity`
before the first step. So a hand-rolled trial measured a marble *dropped from
rest* - 1.6 layout units per second building to 11 - and at that speed it
slides down leg3's 26-degree bank to the inside of the turn, which looked like
a geometry finding and was an initial condition. The bank alone holds a marble
at 14 to 22 units per second there, so below that a marble goes to the inside
whatever the fork looks like.

The `--alone` sweep, which does apply the velocity, is the number above.


CONCLUSION
----------
The named correction is complete and correct. The blocker is upstream of it and
is excluded from this session's scope. **Orange stays off: `routes="blue"` is
still the default and the shipped course is the through route.** Recorded as a
V1 limitation.
