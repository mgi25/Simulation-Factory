LOCAL RAIL BOOSTS - the start-lift escapes, and leg1's trap with them
=====================================================================

Tools: tools/sloped_escape_trace.py (where the field leaves and with what),
       tools/sloped_continuity_check.py (the geometry it leaves on).


1. WHAT THE ESCAPES ACTUALLY ARE
--------------------------------
A loss-site histogram says where a racer left. It does not say whether it went
over a guard, through a floor, or was outside the channel before the instrument
found it - three different repairs. So the trace records the state at the tick
the verdict fires.

96 seeds, 768 racers, the frozen floor-fast start:

Every one is 31 to 46 per cent of a half width *outside* the channel with its
centre near the 0.800 containment, at 18 to 29 layout units per second with 3
to 9 of that lateral, and every vertical speed is negative. **They have gone
over the top of the rail and are falling outside it.** The worst height any
racer reached was 1.510, nearly double the rail.

**The tool's first verdict labels were wrong and said the opposite.** They read
"over the guard" only when the *vertical* test fired, and the vertical slack is
a whole marble diameter - so eleven of twelve escapes were labelled "outside
the wall" while their centres were above the rail and 0.3 outside its face. The
verdict now reports the test that fired and the height against the rail top as
two separate facts.


2. WHAT IT IS NOT
-----------------
`tools/sloped_continuity_check.py` audits the whole chain for the defects
section 4 names, and rules three of them out:

  launch -> leg1: centreline gap 0.0000, floor step -0.0000, bank step 0.00 deg
  leg1   -> leg2: centreline gap 0.0000, floor step +0.0000, bank step 0.00 deg
  leg2   -> leg3: centreline gap 0.0000, floor step +0.0000, bank step 0.00 deg

No run has a grade break over 8 degrees anywhere. So it is not a seam, not a
kerb and not a step. What the audit does find is a 12.1-degree tangent break at
leg2[88] with a 1.77 turn radius under 22 degrees of bank, and one of 6.6
degrees at leg1[92] - the two hairpins, in the two bands the brief names.

**The audit's first speed model was wrong and had to be replaced.** It computed
`tan(bank + wall)` with the sum clamped at 89 degrees, so a positive bank put
the argument at the clamp, `tan(89)` made almost any turn look able to hold
anything, and leg1 reported zero tight samples while marbles were demonstrably
leaving it. It also added the bank to the wall without checking which way the
corner went. It now walks the channel's own profile outward, with the bank's
sign taken from the turn's direction, and reports the height a marble rides to.

That model says the channel holds 27 units/s *in equilibrium* everywhere. So
the escapes are dynamic rather than static: the marbles arrive with 3 to 9
units/s of lateral velocity - from each other, in a clump - and climb past
their equilibrium point ballistically.


3. THE LEVER THAT IS FALSIFIED
------------------------------
The launch site is the one V1.8's start created: its axial catch delivers the
field to launch[0] with 4.26 of drop behind it against the fan's 0.59, so the
field arrives as a clump at about three times the speed. The fan loses **zero**
racers on the launch and the same 5 of 768 at leg1.

So the first thing tried was less arrival energy - a shallower exit chute and a
shallower catch cone, which also lowers the lift. It does not work, and the
failure is total:

  chute  cone   lift   field falls   delivered   escapes
   17     14    3.929      4.259       99.87%      12
   11      9    3.224      3.554        0.00%       0     768 stopped in the chute
    8      6    2.865      3.195        0.00%       0     768 stopped in the chute

**Below about 15 degrees the clump stacks in the chute and none of 768 racers
ever reaches a run.** That is V1.7's own measured floor, confirmed at the higher
arrival speed rather than assumed to have moved. The lift is what the
architecture costs.


4. THE CORRECTION
-----------------
The centreline, the widths, the drops and the bank extremes of every run are
pinned by `sloped.contract` against `physics_layout.json` and the drawn asset,
and `course.check()` has to stay at zero findings - so none of those is
available. The rail's height is not pinned, and it is the surface the marbles
are demonstrably clearing.

`TrackRun.guard_boost` is a window - authored height before sample `a`, eased to
`authored + extra` by `b`, held to `c`, eased back by `d`. Smoothstepped rather
than stepped, because a step in a rail's top is a kerb a marble riding the wall
trips on. Only the points above the lip's crown move, and only upward: the lip
and the cradle are the running surface and are left exactly as the asset draws
them.

  launch  (0.50, 12, 24, 70, 84)
  leg1    (0.50, 46, 58, 100, 110)
  leg2    (0.50, 60, 72, 106, 116)

Measured, 96 seeds each:

  configuration                       escapes   rate     launch   leg1
  authored 0.26 rail                    12     1.562%      7        5
  +0.34 over the narrower windows        6     0.781%      4        2
  +0.50 over these windows               1     0.130%      1        0

**And the containment check had to move with the collider.** `run.containment`
is a scalar; the boost is per sample. A check reading the scalar while the
collider carried the boost would book a contained marble as an escape and the
histogram would say the repair had done nothing. Both `sloped.race` and
`sloped.startlab` now read `containment_at(index)`.

The intermediate +0.34 row is why the windows were widened as well as raised:
one of its six remaining escapes was at leg1[59], outside leg1's window
entirely, and four of the others were within 0.10 of the raised rail's top.


5. THE RENDER CARRIES THE SAME RAILS
------------------------------------
`v2_track.gd` grows an additive `guard_boost` option and `course_machine.gd`
passes the same three windows. Additive is deliberate: without the key every
existing build gets the authored 0.26 rail, which is what keeps the earlier
labs' committed frames reproducing.

A rail the physics has and the render does not is one a viewer watches a marble
bounce off nothing on. `tests/test_sloped_guards.py` parses the GDScript
constant and compares it to `sloped.course.GUARD_BOOSTS` number for number,
because the alternative is a third file neither tree can import.
