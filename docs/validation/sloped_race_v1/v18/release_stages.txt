THE FULL-FLOOR RELEASE - section 11's staged validation, in order
=================================================================

Tools: tools/sloped_floor_check.py (geometry, before any marble),
       tools/sloped_floor_trace.py (stages a, b, c),
       tools/sloped_start_bench.py --start-kind floor (stage d).

READ THE LAST SECTION FIRST. Sections 0 to D below were measured against the
**stadium dish** the release was first built over, and the dish is superseded:
the numbers that describe the *release* carry over unchanged, but every number
that describes the catch - the 0.89 chute, the 0.70 dish depth, the 2.81 lift,
the stage-D fairness table - belongs to a catch that no longer exists. The
final section is why, and it is the session's main finding. The staged run was
repeated on the cone and is quoted there.


0. GEOMETRY, BEFORE ANY MARBLE
------------------------------
Six slats at pitch 0.900, 0.10 thick, three boxes each sharing one hinge,
sweeping 90 degrees in 0.20s, hinged on their upstream edges.

  18 boxes, ONE (release, duration, sweep) triple between them
  5025 points of the chamber disc sampled: every one over a slat
  seam at rest 0.000, at 90 deg 0.800 = 1.404 marble diameters
  worst narrowing anywhere in the sweep  +0.000000
  worst crescent between a slat end and the wall  +0.0000
  a marble against the wall: 0 panel-angles push into it
  slats' deepest sweep 0.951 at 87 deg; a marble on the dish reaches 1.110
  0 points of the chamber disc are off the dish
  the fall 1.839 to 2.339, landing 22.7 to 25.6 layout units per second
  rebound at restitution 0.15 rises 0.074 against a 0.78 rim

Three defects were found here rather than by running marbles, and each is now
asserted in tests/test_sloped_trapdoor.py:

**The seam is `pitch - thickness`, not `pitch`.** A slat rotated by an angle
has a horizontal footprint of `(t/2) sin + (w/2) cos`, so the clear gap is
`pitch (1 - cos) - thickness sin` to 90 degrees and `pitch (1 + cos) -
thickness sin` past it. It **peaks at 90** at `pitch - thickness`: beyond
that the slat folds back over its own hinge, the hinge edge becomes its
downstream extreme, and the seam closes again. Built at a 120-degree sweep on
a closed form that had the fold's sign wrong, the final seam measured 0.299
instead of the predicted 1.070. The check tool walks the real box corners
every degree, which is what caught it.

That one term set the slat count:

  slats   pitch   seam = pitch - t   diameters
    8     0.675       0.575            1.01     a marble rubs both sides
    7     0.771       0.671            1.18     inside the catchable band
    6     0.900       0.800            1.40     passes freely
    5     1.080       0.980            1.72     and 0.18 more well depth

**The well has to clear a marble standing on the dish, not the dish.** The
first build set the dish 0.16 below the slats' deepest sweep and the geometry
check reported a healthy +0.159 clearance - to the *surface*. A marble resting
on the dish has its top 0.285 above that surface, which put it 0.023 into the
hanging plate. See stage A below. The well now carries a whole marble
diameter and the check asks the right question.

**The chute is 0.89 long, and that is geometry rather than a choice.** The
dish's cap has to cover the whole chamber disc from its own centre, which
forces `CATCH_HALF >= |CHAMBER_Z - CATCH_Z| + R_WALL` and therefore puts the
spillway at or downstream of the chamber's far edge, `CHAMBER_Z + R_WALL =
3.00`. The launch entry is at 3.94. A spillway upstream of the region it
drains would leave part of the dish sloping away from the exit.


A. ONE STATIONARY MARBLE, 33 CHAMBER POSITIONS
----------------------------------------------
Fifteen positions from the centre to 0.95 of the free radius at eight
bearings, on seams and on hinge lines; then eighteen more **hard against the
wall** at every 20 degrees of bearing, added after stage C found a failure that
0.95 of the way out could not reproduce.

  first build      9 of 15 delivered, 6 stopped on the dish
  well corrected  33 of 33 delivered, 0 not delivered, 0 left the machine

The six were the marble-diameter defect above. Traced tick by tick, one landed
cleanly at (-0.22, +0.01), settled exactly on the dish surface, and then rolled
back and forth in x between -0.54 and -0.15 for five seconds while z crept
0.016. It was resting on the top edge of a hanging slat.

**And the tool's own verdicts were wrong before that.** It called a marble 34
units down leg1 "left the machine" because it was below the dish's lip, and a
marble stopped on the dish "still in the chamber" because it was above the
dish's rim. Both readings flatter the mechanism. There are now three states -
never released, stopped on the dish, delivered - and the middle one is the
interesting failure.


B. EIGHT STATIONARY MARBLES, RELEASED TOGETHER
----------------------------------------------
Two groups: four at 0.90 of the free radius on the cardinals plus four inner at
0.45 on the diagonals; and eight sitting on the slat seams.

  ring and inner   8 of 8 delivered
  on the seams     8 of 8 delivered

Release times 6.27 to 6.43s against a floor that opens at 6.10 - a spread of
0.17s over sixteen marbles, which is the position independence showing up as a
number rather than as an argument.


C. THE REAL FIELD, 12 THEN 24 SEEDS
-----------------------------------
The bays, the gate, the apron and the rotor, with the shipped downstream.

  before the paddles were lifted   94 of 96 delivered (97.92%)
  after                           192 of 192 delivered (100.00%)

The two were both at radius 2.412 against a free radius of 2.415, and neither
was reproducible from a resting position: 18 stage-A marbles at the exact
radius limit at every 20 degrees all delivered. Traced, the pocket had three
walls - the chamber wall, the **top edge of a hanging slat** (a 0.10-wide
horizontal ledge at the hinge line, 0.05 below where the floor was), and a
**stopped rotor blade** beside it. The marble sat dead still, velocity 0.00,
for seven seconds.

Two of those three walls are structural: the hinge stays where it is, so a
hinged trapdoor always leaves a fin. The blade is not. With the full-floor
release the paddles have no further job once the mixing is over, so they rise
0.68 - a blade's underside sits 0.03 above the floor and a marble standing on
it reaches 0.57 - starting a quarter of the way into the settle and finishing
0.45s before the floor opens. `Rotor.lift_at` is a smoothstep in the tick like
everything else, and `ShuffleChamber` keeps `ROTOR_LIFT = 0.0` so V1.7's
outlet, which does need its blades where they are, is unchanged.


D. 96 SEEDS IN THE START LAB
----------------------------
768 racers, the full instrument - containment, stalls, trailing, ranks.

  lost      7  (0.911%)   leg1[70..79%] x5, leg1[80..89%] x2
  stuck     1  (0.130%)   start[0..9%] x1
  trailing 19  (2.474%)
  delivered           98.958%
  incomplete trials    0

**Seven of the eight losses are the pre-existing `leg1[70..89%]` trap**, which
V1.7 recorded as its own remaining issue and which the shipped fan has too. The
start's own contribution is one racer in 768, or 0.130%.

So the throughput gate is met on the release's own account and the combined
figure is short of 99.5% because of a downstream trap that section 18 owns.


WHAT THE RELEASE COST, AND WHAT IT DID NOT
------------------------------------------
Removing the outlet removed three things with it, and each is a simplification:

  the chamber floor is flat and level      V1.7 needed 7 degrees of cone to
                                           persuade the field toward a hole,
                                           and measured that a floor steep
                                           enough to deliver packs the field
                                           against the closed outlet and
                                           recreates the taper's centre bias
  blade clearance is one number            0.030 everywhere, against 0.030 at
                                           the tip and 0.208 at the root
  free radial travel is the whole disc     4.24 marble diameters, against 2.07
                                           either side of a gate ring

`START_LIFT` is re-derived at **2.81**, against V1.7's 2.14 and the radial
start's 4.30. The whole of the increase is the marble diameter the well has to
carry, and it is the price of a hinged floor.


THE CATCH IS WHERE THE SELECTION WENT
=====================================

The release is provably position-independent - eighteen boxes, one (release,
duration, sweep) triple, nothing in `FloorPanel` reads a position, an id, a bay
or a rank - and it delivers 100% of the field. It did not make the start fair.

Two catches were built and measured in the same instrument, over 96 seeds each,
behind the same chamber and the same downstream:

  catch                      delivered   exit span   slot r   centre r   lift
  stadium dish, rim notch      98.958%      2.479     +0.264    +0.886    2.81
  cone on the axis, throat     98.177%      1.208     -0.074    -0.928    3.93

and for scale, in the same instrument:

  fan, as shipped (V1.3)       99.35%       2.927     -0.387    +0.574      -
  rotor + central outlet (V1.7) 88.93%      2.200     -0.206    -0.182    2.14

The finding is general enough to state as one:

> A catch with one exit orders the field by path length to that exit. So a
> position-independent release does not remove the selection - it **moves** it
> to the catch, and the statistic that then decides the race is "how far from
> the exit was this marble when the floor went". The catch's exit therefore has
> to sit wherever the mixing has actually equalised the field.

The rotor equalises exactly one coordinate, and the pre-release table says
which. With the paddles stopped, `bay -> radius` is -0.065 and
`|bay-3.5| -> radius` is -0.202, while the bearing still carries the bay at a
per-bay concentration of 0.358 against a 0.090 noise floor. Distance to a point
**on the chamber's axis** is the radius. Distance to a notch on the rim is a
function of radius *and* bearing, and it reads back the one thing the chamber
did not erase - amplified, because the notch is 2.75 downstream of a chamber
only 2.70 across, so the dominant term is the bay's residual z.

That is also why V1.7's central outlet was, in hindsight, the fairest catch
this tree has built. Its problem was throughput and never fairness.

Moving the exit onto the axis took the **exit rank span from 2.479 to 1.208
places** - less than half V1.7's 2.200 and under half the shipped fan's 2.927,
the best figure this project has measured - and the slot correlation from
+0.264 to -0.074, which is indistinguishable from none.

The stadium dish is **not** kept in the tree. The basin is kept because it is
one line to switch back to; this is not - the dish and the cone differ in every
term of the height chain, in the mesh, in the probes and in the lift, so a
switch would be a second module pretending to be a flag. Its numbers are above
and its report is `startlab_floor_tuned_96.json`.


WHAT THE COST OF THE AXIAL CATCH IS
-----------------------------------
`START_LIFT` goes 2.81 to **3.93**, against V1.7's 2.14 and the radial start's
4.30, and the containment losses go 8 to 14 of 768 with it:

  leg1[70..79%] x6, leg1[80..89%] x2     the pre-existing trap, in both
  launch[30..39%] x3, launch[40..49%] x2  new, and the lift is why
  leg1[50..59%] x1

The five launch losses are the mechanism V1.7 already measured on its own chute
grade: a steeper feed puts the field onto the launch's banked plunge faster and
it leaves over the guard. Nothing is stuck and nothing is unreleased - the
start's own contribution to the loss column is **zero** - so this is a
downstream repair of the kind section 18 owns rather than a start defect.


AND WHAT REMAINS, MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSUMED
----------------------------------------------
The centre correlation is -0.928 and the span is 1.208 places, and those two
numbers are not in conflict: a correlation measures shape and a span measures
size. Grouped by distance from the middle of the pan:

  |bay - 3.5|   mean exit rank
      0.5            5.172        bays 3 and 4
      1.5            4.589
      2.5            4.130
      3.5            4.110        bays 0 and 7

**Six of the eight bays sit within 0.5 places of each other.** What is left is
a penalty of about one place on the two centre bays, and its seed is visible in
the chamber: `|bay-3.5| -> drain distance` is -0.202 there, with the same sign.
Bays 3 and 4 are the only ones with no lateral distance to travel across the
apron, so they enter first and fastest, cross the chamber and come to rest
furthest from its axis - and an axial catch reads exactly that.

So the residual is the chamber's, not the release's and not the catch's
position. The release is falsified as *the* cause of start bias and confirmed
as a working mechanism; the remaining bias is a small entry-order effect that
2.39 revolutions of stirring does not erase.
