PRE-RELEASE CHAMBER STATE - what is left of the starting bay before anything opens
==================================================================================

Instrument: tools/sloped_chamber_state.py, sloped/chamberstate.py.
Each trial runs the rotor start to the tick before the release opens and reads
the field in the start module's own frame. No exit statistics are involved.

Sampled at `ShuffleChamber.gate_time`, read off the module rather than typed.

  bay->(x,z)   the magnitude of the (bay->x, bay->z) correlation vector.
               Invariant to how far the rotor carried the field round, which
               the two components separately are not.
  conc         per-bay bearing concentration (mean resultant length), averaged
               over the eight bays. 0 = that bay's racers end up anywhere;
               1 = that bay always ends up at one bearing. The noise floor for
               a genuinely uniform angle is 0.886/sqrt(n): 0.064 at 192 seeds,
               0.090 at 96.
  sector R     bay -> bearing folded into one blade pitch. V1.7's fixed-phase
               defect shows here and nowhere else.

Mardia's coefficient tops out at about 0.81, not 1.0, when the bays are spread
round the whole circle - the bay index is deterministic in the bearing but not
monotone in any linear embedding of a circle it goes all the way round. So the
measured 0.22 to 0.53 below are fractions of 0.81. Pinned in
tests/test_sloped_chamberstate.py.
  cyclic       |2p-1| for the fraction of bay triples whose cyclic order round
               the chamber matches their cyclic order across the pan.
               0 = broken; 1 = the necklace merely rotated.


1. THE INSTRUMENT REPRODUCES V1.7's FIXED-PHASE DEFECT INDEPENDENTLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
192 seeds each, everything else at V1.7's shipped rotor values.

  configuration        bay->(x,z)   conc    sector R   cyclic
  rotor, fixed phase       0.446      -        0.529    0.026
  rotor, seed phase        0.406      -        0.026    0.002

V1.7 traced the fixed-phase defect one marble at a time and found four parking
bearings 90 degrees apart. The sector column is that finding, measured over
1536 racers: 0.529 against 0.026, a twentyfold difference in the one statistic
that can see it. The seed-derived global phase is confirmed.


2. THE CYCLIC ORDER IS COMPLETELY DESTROYED, AND THAT IS NOT ENOUGH
-------------------------------------------------------------------
At the shipped configuration, over 10752 triples, cyclic order retained is
0.002 - indistinguishable from a random cyclic permutation. The rotor really
does break the necklace.

But every bay still has its own preferred arc of the chamber:

  slot  bearing  concentration        (192 seeds, settle 1.20)
    0    111.6      0.560
    1     57.0      0.714
    2    348.5      0.757
    3      2.2      0.638
    4    354.4      0.551
    5     59.8      0.619
    6    151.3      0.563
    7    219.1      0.577

Mean 0.622 against a 0.064 noise floor - ten times it - and the eight means
span 230.6 degrees rather than clustering. The field as a whole concentrates at
only 0.253, *less* than any individual bay, which is the signature that
separates the two cases: a field that all ends up in one arc because the inlet
is at one bearing is fair, and a field whose eight bays each prefer a different
arc is the sectoring five earlier topologies died of.

Both statements are consistent. A concentration of 0.62 is a circular standard
deviation of about 56 degrees, and the bay means are about 33 degrees apart, so
neighbouring bays overlap enough for their pairwise order to be a coin flip
while each still has a strong absolute preference.


3. MIXING DURATION DOES NOTHING, AND THE REASON IS MECHANICAL
-------------------------------------------------------------
96 seeds each, settle 1.20, rotor running from tick zero.

  mix     turns   bay->(x,z)   bay->x    bay->z    conc
  1.50     1.19      0.354     -0.291    -0.203   0.622
  3.00     2.39      0.380     -0.292    +0.244     -
  4.50     3.58      0.370     +0.173    +0.327   0.624
  9.00     7.16      0.409        -         -     0.598

Six times the mixing changes nothing. `bay->x` and `bay->z` swing sign while
their *magnitude* holds at 0.35 to 0.41 and the concentration holds at 0.60.
That is a bulk rotation, not a residual order: the two components are the
projections of one vector that the rotor carries round, and a longer run turns
it further without shrinking it.

The mechanism: a racer's transport angle is `rate * (stop_time - its own
arrival time)`, and the eight bays arrive at systematically different times
because they have different distances to travel across the apron. So the bay is
written into the final bearing as an arrival-time *difference*, and a longer
run adds the same constant to every racer and cancels none of it.


4. WHAT DOES WORK: HOLD THE ROTOR UNTIL THE FIELD IS IN
-------------------------------------------------------
If the differential is in the residence time, remove the differential. The
rotor holds still through entry and turns only for the mixing interval, so
every racer sees the same number of degrees of rotor.

  configuration                   bay->(x,z)   conc    sector R  cyclic  x span
  running from tick 0, mix 1.5       0.406     0.622     0.026   0.002   1.570
  held, mix 1.5                      0.302     0.410     0.075   0.015   1.084
  held, mix 3.0                      0.218     0.361       -     0.028     -
  held, mix 4.5                      0.274     0.384       -     0.027     -

192 seeds for the first two, 96 for the last two.

Held, the residual falls by a quarter and the concentration by a third - and
now that the residual is entry *position* rather than entry *time*, mixing
starts to bite: mix 3.0 takes it to 0.218 and 0.361. Mix 4.5 is no better,
so 3.0 is where it flattens.

Per-bay concentrations, held at mix 1.5: bays 3 to 7 are 0.22 to 0.35, close to
noise for 192 seeds; bays 0, 1 and 2 are 0.73, 0.59 and 0.52. The outer western
bays are the last to arrive and are still near the inlet when the rotor starts.


5. THE SETTLE, WHICH IS A SEPARATE RESIDUAL
-------------------------------------------
96 to 192 seeds each, rotor running from tick zero, mix 1.50.

  settle   bay->radius   bay->speed   radial rho   mean speed
   0.55       -0.343       -0.357       -0.389      0.644
   1.20       -0.174       -0.040       -0.119      0.192
   2.00       -0.149       +0.027       +0.026      0.080

At V1.7's 0.55 the field has not finished settling and half of it is still
moving: bays 0 to 3 at 0.81 to 1.34 layout units per second, bays 4 to 7 at
0.24 to 0.37. That residual energy carries the bay. 1.20 removes essentially
all of it and 2.00 buys nothing further, so 1.20 is the answer.

This is a different residual from the azimuthal one and it responds to a
different knob, which is why both were measured separately.


6. THE ANSWER TO SECTION 3
--------------------------
A strong systematic bay -> position relationship remained at the configuration
V1.7 reported, so section 3's second branch applies and three small changes
were justified by the measurement rather than scanned:

    SETTLE_SECONDS   0.55 -> 1.20     the field had not stopped moving
    rotor held for entry              equalises the residence time
    MIX_SECONDS      1.50 -> 3.00     now that the residual is spatial

Together: bay->(x,z) 0.406 -> 0.218, per-bay bearing concentration 0.62 -> 0.36,
mean chamber speed 0.64 -> under 0.2, cyclic order still broken.

**The residual is halved and it is not zero.** A release that does not read
position will carry whatever is left faithfully downstream, so this is the
ceiling the release can be measured against - not a clean slate.


7. THE ROTOR RATE, WHICH ONLY A TRAPDOOR MAKES USABLE
-----------------------------------------------------
The third knob section 3 allows, tested last because V1.7 could not use it: a
fast rotor centrifuges the field away from a central outlet and starves the
delivery - six of eight racers simply stayed put when its gate dropped. A
full-floor release does not care where the field is, so the rate is free.

And what it buys is measured on the statistic an *axial* catch actually reads,
which is the marble's own chamber radius. 96 seeds each, held for entry, mix
3.00, settle 1.20, the `floor` chamber with its flat level floor:

  rate   turns   bay -> radius   |bay-3.5| -> radius   drain span   in chamber
   5.0    2.39       -0.065            -0.202            0.375         100%
   9.0    4.30       +0.006            -0.119            0.317         100%
  13.0    6.21       +0.019            -0.047            0.419         100%

**The centre-shaped residual falls from -0.202 to -0.047** - four times down,
and it is the residual the whole session's remaining bias is made of. The plain
`bay -> radius` correlation is already at zero by 9 rad/s.

Nothing is thrown out of the chamber at 13 rad/s, which is the property the
rotor was moved out of the channel to get: the wall contains the field, the
tip-to-wall clearance is 0.150 at every blade angle, and a gap with no angle in
it cannot close on a marble. The earlier in-channel wheel put 80.2% of the
field in the stuck column at 12 rad/s for exactly the opposite reason.

Note what the rate does *not* fix. The per-bay bearing concentration is 0.358
at 5 rad/s, 0.391 at 9 and 0.384 at 13 - flat, like the mixing duration before
it. A rotor is a rotation whatever its speed, and the bay's bearing residual is
not something more of it removes. What changes is the *radius*, and the radius
is the coordinate an axial catch reads. So this is not the rotor mixing better;
it is the rotor equalising the one number the catch asks about.
