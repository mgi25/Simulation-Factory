## 1. Executive Vision and Target State
Simulation Factory is to evolve into an AI-native entertainment company whose core business is discovering, developing, producing and improving simulation-based YouTube content. The current Race and Fight systems are the first two format families, not the final identity of the company. The company must continually search for Format #3, #4 and beyond while improving existing formats.
The human owner operates as CEO. The CEO should not become the task router, researcher, engineer, reviewer and analyst for every job. The organization underneath the CEO must coordinate those functions, surface only material decisions, and remain accountable through evidence, tests, budgets and measurable outcomes.

North-Star Objective

Long-term profitable audience growth through original, highly watchable simulation entertainment, produced by a continuously improving technical and organizational system.

## 2. Founding Principles and Non-Negotiable Rules
| Principle | Meaning |
| CEO authority | The human CEO owns the mission, major strategic direction and explicitly reserved decisions. |
| Evidence over confidence | Agents may be confident and still be wrong. Important decisions require measurable evidence, tests, research, benchmarks or explicit uncertainty. |
| Originality over cloning | References are used to extract principles, mechanics and viewer psychology. The company does not reproduce another creator's protected assets, branding, music, exact course or distinctive presentation. |
| Quality subject to resources | The target is the best practical result under current constraints, not endless polishing or token minimization that reduces quality. |
| Minimum relevant context | Every task receives only the context required to solve it well. |
| Software before LLM | Deterministic computation, tests, parsers, scripts, caching and retrieval are preferred whenever judgment is not required. |
| One source of truth | Facts are stored canonically and retrieved; they are not repeatedly copied into prompts and documents. |
| One experimental variable where possible | Do not change many major factors simultaneously when the purpose is to learn which change affected viewer or engineering outcomes. |
| Rollback always | Code, organizational structure, prompts, employees, technology and important data changes must be recoverable. |
| No failed work without learning | A failed video, prototype or experiment should produce a documented learning unless failure was purely operational and uninformative. |
| No permanent sacred technology | Godot, a model provider, a framework, a workflow or an employee role may be replaced when evidence supports a better alternative. |
| Autonomy with boundaries | The company may explore aggressively in sandboxes, but production changes and high-impact actions pass the appropriate gate. |

### Constitutional protections
Never publish without required QC and authorization.
Never silently change physics, race outcome, camera, environment and edit simultaneously when the task is an experiment intended to isolate one cause.
Never spend above the active AI/compute budget without escalation.
Never let a newly created employee immediately receive production-write authority.
Never allow a technology migration to be justified only by novelty or preference; require benchmarks, migration cost and expected break-even.
Never allow production modules to depend on the company-management subsystem.
Never treat an unvalidated hypothesis as a fact.
Never permanently destroy important company knowledge, employee history, experiments or decisions without retention policy and auditability.

## 3. Bootstrap Constraint: No Subagents and Minimum-Token Operation

Non-Negotiable Bootstrap Rule

During current development and during normal agent execution, no session or agent may spawn subagents, child agents, parallel helper agents or autonomous nested agent teams. This applies to Codex, Claude Code and comparable coding/agent tools. Each invoked agent/session must perform its assigned task directly. Any future change to this rule requires an explicit CEO policy change backed by measured cost/benefit.

The reason is operational efficiency: subagent systems can multiply context, reasoning calls and duplicated work. The company is being designed to work under a Plus-plan constraint first. Therefore the architecture must achieve strong results without depending on unlimited inference.

### Bootstrap efficiency policy
| Tier | Preferred mechanism | Typical examples |
| A — No AI | Deterministic software | Metrics, math, git diff, tests, video metadata, file inspection, token accounting, frame measurements. |
| B — Retrieval | Cache / knowledge lookup | Known architectural facts, past decisions, prior experiment outcomes, module contracts. |
| C — Small reasoning | Single concise agent call | Classification, routing, short synthesis, simple review. |
| D — Specialist reasoning | One specialist with bounded context | Physics diagnosis, camera critique, content analysis, code review. |
| E — Deep reasoning | One strong reasoning session | Architecture, difficult debugging, major research synthesis, new format design. |
| F — Multi-perspective review | Sequential named reviews, not subagents | Rare major decisions. Each reviewer is invoked separately and writes a compact artifact. |

### Key token metrics
Tokens per accepted deliverable, not simply tokens per request.
First-pass success rate.
Retry count and reason.
Context size and proportion actually used.
Duplicate retrieval rate.
Unnecessary tool-call rate.
Model strength used versus task complexity.
Cost of reasoning compared with value of the accepted output.
Recurring facts that should be promoted to a compact knowledge capsule.
A 5,000-token task that succeeds once can be cheaper than five 2,000-token failed iterations. The efficiency target is therefore minimum total resource cost at the required quality level.

### Management design
Managers exist to compress information, resolve normal conflicts, enforce budgets and route exceptions upward.
Managers do not continuously poll employees. Management is event-driven.

## 9. AI Infrastructure and Token-Efficiency Platform

### 9.1 Model/router policy
The strongest reasoning mode must not be used automatically. Task complexity, risk, context size and expected value determine the minimum sufficient model/capability.

### 9.2 Context compiler
Before an agent runs, a context compiler should assemble only the relevant task specification, module capsule, affected files/diff, required tests, recent decisions and relevant experiment evidence.

Example

A camera task should receive the camera contract, affected camera files, replay/course interfaces, relevant camera tests and recent camera findings. It should not receive finance documents, Fight balance history or the entire repository.

### 9.3 Retrieval before reasoning
If the company already knows a fact, retrieve it. Do not pay an agent to rediscover it. Repeated retrieval of the same large source indicates the source should be compressed into a durable knowledge capsule.

### 9.4 Structured artifacts, not agent chatter
Researcher → research_report.json
Creative   → concept_brief.json
Engineer   → implementation_report.json
Reviewer   → validation_report.json
Analytics  → postmortem.json
Manager    → decision_packet.json
Agents should not hold open-ended conversations with one another. Work is handed off using compact structured artifacts. Sequential review is allowed; nested subagent spawning is not.

### 9.5 AI observability
Input/output tokens or comparable usage units where available.
Model/reasoning level.
Number of tool calls.
Retry count.
Task duration.
Accepted/rejected status.
Reason for rejection.
Context sources supplied.
Estimated wasted context.
Cache hit/miss.
Escalation frequency.

### 9.6 Cost policy
During Bootstrap Mode, paid API services are optional and should not be required for normal company operation. When channel revenue exists, API capacity can be added under a defined budget, potentially as a percentage of trailing revenue. Increased spend must be tied to measurable throughput, quality or revenue impact.

## 11. Reference Acquisition, Reverse Engineering, Originality and Superiority
A promising external video may become a reference case. If the company has the legal/rightful ability to retain a local copy, it can do so. Otherwise it should retain the URL, metadata, thumbnails/stills where permitted, notes and extracted research artifacts rather than build an unauthorized download workflow.

### Reference-case analysis
Hook: how quickly the premise is understood.
Viewer commitment: whether the viewer picks a competitor or makes a prediction.
Core loop: prediction → event → consequence → new prediction.
Competitive change rate and suspense.
Visual hierarchy and mobile readability.
Camera language and spatial continuity.
Color/material/environment depth.
Audio feedback and payoff.
Runtime and pacing.
Production complexity.
Repeatability as a format.
Strengths, weaknesses and opportunities to create something original and stronger.

### Reference Superiority Contract
Development should not begin with the vague instruction 'make it better.' For each reference, the team should state measurable or reviewable dimensions in which our product must match or exceed the reference while remaining original.
| Dimension | Reference baseline | Our target | Validation method |
| Hook comprehension | Observed / estimated | Faster or equally clear | Viewer review / timing |
| Competitive change | Observed event rhythm | Higher meaningful event density where appropriate | Replay event analysis |
| Mobile readability | Observed subject scale | Meet internal pixel/readability gate | Rendered frame measurement |
| Continuity | Reference camera/edit | No hidden competitive discontinuity unless format explicitly allows it | Timeline/replay audit |
| Visual depth | Reference quality | Equal or stronger hierarchy/depth | Art/QC review + frame metrics |
| Originality | External implementation | Distinct mechanics/assets/identity | Originality review |
| Audio | Reference feedback | Clearer mechanical/action feedback | Audio QC |
| Repeatability | Unknown/observed | Defined reusable format system | Format contract |

## 13. Per-Video Intelligence Dossier and Viewer Psychology
Every significant video should start with a documented Video Intelligence Dossier so that production is based on a hypothesis about viewer behavior rather than random iteration.
| Dossier section | Questions answered |
| Audience goal | Who is this for and what should they feel/do? |
| Previous performance | What did recent channel videos teach us? |
| External references | Which patterns are worth learning from? |
| Current weakness | Where is the present format lacking? |
| Hypothesis | What specific change should improve watchability? |
| Hook | What does the viewer understand in the first seconds? |
| Commitment | What makes the viewer choose/predict/care? |
| Middle | What repeatedly changes the answer to 'who is winning/what happens next'? |
| Payoff | What outcome is the viewer waiting for? |
| Visual plan | How will the product remain readable and attractive on a phone? |
| Camera plan | How does the viewer stay oriented while seeing upcoming threats? |
| Audio plan | Which actions receive feedback and escalation? |
| Experiment definition | What changed and what stayed locked? |
| Reference superiority | Which specific dimensions must improve? |
| Cost/risk | What will it cost and what can fail? |

### Viewer psychology timeline
0-1 s    What is happening?
1-3 s    What/whom do I choose?
3-6 s    What can change?
6-10 s   What am I waiting to discover?
10-15 s  Has the situation meaningfully changed?
Finish   Was the payoff worth the wait?
Exact timing depends on the video, but the principle is persistent: motion alone is not sufficient. The viewer needs changing stakes, uncertainty, readability and payoff.

## 15. High-Level Software Engineering and AI-Friendly Architecture
Simulation Factory must be treated as a serious software platform, not a collection of scripts made only for the next Short. Architecture should optimize simultaneously for runtime quality, maintainability, testing and future AI comprehension cost.

### AI code readability principles
Prefer cohesive small modules over giant files that force large context loads.
Prefer explicit interfaces over hidden cross-module state.
Keep docs close to the module and short enough to retrieve cheaply.
Avoid duplicating the same architectural truth across many files.
Use stable, descriptive naming that lets retrieval find the correct code quickly.
Keep tests discoverable by module.
Separate physics, replay, camera, environment, materials, analytics and corporate orchestration.
Refactor only when the measured future value exceeds migration risk/cost.

### Technical debt and organizational debt
The company must track both. Technical debt includes duplication, giant modules, brittle dependencies and missing tests. Organizational debt includes duplicated employees, bloated prompts, unnecessary approvals, stale knowledge and managers that add coordination overhead without improving outcomes.

## 25. Definition of Perfect and Reference Superiority Contract
The company frequently aims for a 'perfect' output. To prevent infinite polishing, perfect must be operationally defined.

Definition of Perfect

No known critical defect; required tests and QC pass; mobile viewing is readable; performance stays within budget; viewer-experience targets are met or evidence-backed; originality requirements pass; no unresolved high-risk issue remains; and the result represents the best practical quality achievable under the current resource/time constraints.

### Kill conditions
Every major experiment or R&D effort should define stop conditions before starting. Examples include unacceptable jam rate, development cost exceeding a threshold, failure to improve the target metric, severe readability regression or migration cost exceeding expected value.

## 28. Development Rules for Codex, Claude Code and Other Coding Agents

Mandatory Development Rule

No coding session may use subagents, child agents, autonomous worker swarms or nested delegation during Bootstrap Mode. The session must work directly from the supplied task and bounded context. If a tool has a default subagent feature, it must be disabled or explicitly avoided.

### Session contract
Receive one clearly bounded objective.
Read only the minimum relevant repository context first.
Do not spawn subagents.
Do not independently broaden scope unless required to satisfy correctness; document any unavoidable expansion.
Prefer existing scripts/tests/contracts over rediscovering architecture.
Make the smallest coherent change that solves the task.
Run relevant tests/benchmarks.
Record what changed and what remained unchanged.
Produce a compact handoff artifact.
Stop rather than invent additional work once acceptance criteria are met.

### When more perspectives are required
Invoke separate specialist sessions sequentially with compact artifacts. Example: Architect review → Engineer implementation → Reviewer validation. These are independent calls coordinated by the top-level workflow, not subagents spawned inside another agent.

## 29. Acceptance Criteria for Company OS v1
Company OS v1 is considered usable when the following capabilities work end-to-end without requiring a multi-agent swarm:
A task can be represented in a standard schema with owner, scope, budget and success criteria.
The correct employee can be selected from the capability registry.
The context compiler can provide bounded context instead of the full repository.
No-subagent policy is present in all relevant agent/session contracts.
AI/resource telemetry records enough information to identify expensive/repeated workflows.
Employees produce standardized handoff artifacts.
Company knowledge distinguishes facts, hypotheses, decisions and time-sensitive research.
A new video can move from research dossier to experiment to production/QC to postmortem.
A capability gap can trigger a proposed hire without automatically granting production permissions.
A candidate employee can be benchmarked in shadow mode.
A technical/organizational change is versioned and reversible.
The CEO can receive a concise decision packet without reading all underlying specialist output.
Current Race/Fight production remains functional and independent of company runtime.

## Closing Statement
Simulation Factory should be developed as both a high-quality simulation/content platform and the operating environment of a lean AI-native company. The immediate constraint is deliberate scarcity: a Plus-plan-first workflow, no subagents, no always-on swarm, minimum relevant context and maximum reuse of deterministic tooling and durable knowledge.
As the channel grows, the company may gain additional paid API/compute capacity, new departments, new technologies and new content format families. That growth should be earned through evidence and revenue rather than assumed at the beginning.
The final target is a company that can research opportunities, create original formats stronger than their references on defined dimensions, engineer them to a professional standard, publish and analyze them, improve its own workforce and architecture, and present the CEO with only the decisions that truly require human judgment.

Founding Operating Principle

Use agents for judgment. Use software for deterministic work. Use evidence for decisions. Use the minimum sufficient context. Preserve reversibility. Learn from every release. Improve both the product and the company.
